A collection of class projects and teaching experiences from my time at MIT, Stanford, and EPFL. These were formative experiences — lots of late nights, teamwork, and the kind of hands-on learning that sticks with you.

## Academic Projects

### ME218: Smart Product Design (Stanford M.S. Depth, 2012–2013)

Three-quarter, project-based graduate course on prototyping embedded systems. Each quarter concluded with an intensive final project (often with a quirky theme) completed in teams of four.

<img src="/assets/img/218Bbot.jpg" alt="ME218 arcade game bot" width="300"/>

#### Interactive Arcade Game

Two player, Batman-themed game that pitted opponents against each other as they quickly hit flashing LEDs. Game startup included triggering an IR sensor with a "secret code" then arousing an accelerometer worn by the user as a "warm up."

- [Project website](http://combatbybatdog.weebly.com/)
- [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9w4HHpkAdsU&feature=youtu.be)

#### Autonomous "Battleship" Competition

Constructed an autonomous robot to navigate a 10'x10' playing field, shooting opponent's targets in a final tournament.

- [Project website](http://theflyingdutchmanbattlebot.weebly.com/)
- [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hMokzTTTHaQ&feature=youtu.be)

#### Wirelessly Controlled Rover

Designed and constructed a remote-controlled robot to handle wireless commands using an event-driven software framework and SPI communication between PIC16F690 chips.

- [Project website](http://theedcarryerexperience.weebly.com/)
- [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gW6jbdK065M)

### ME2.750: Precision Machine Design (MIT, Fall 2011)

<img src="/assets/img/SutureTie.png" alt="SutureTie device" width="300"/>

I worked as part of a student team of five, advised by a surgeon, to develop the SutureTie — a hand-held device that used zip-tie sutures to occlude blood flow through discrete sections of the liver. Our team produced a proof-of-concept prototype demonstrated on an ex-vivo porcine liver.

The project provided experience defining functional requirements, combing through prior art, working with regular input from our assigned surgeon, and sitting in on a liver resection.

- [Poster presentation](/assets/docs/DMDposter_RevD.pdf)

## Teaching

### EPFL CS-432: Computational Motor Control (2020)

Supporting TA helping students through Python assignments. Material pertained to the simulation of muscle models and central pattern generator based controllers of articulated robot and animal models.

### Stanford ME112: Mechanical Systems Design (2016 & 2014)

I was a teaching assistant under my advisor Mark Cutkosky for winter quarter 2014, and head TA winter 2016. The class was a mix of problem sets, structured labs, and two projects. We taught gears, DC motors, linkage design, and efficiencies through the system. My favorite parts of the class were the "Aha" moments as students realized how different pieces of analysis taught in lectures were finally applied to trace energy flow from battery to useful motion in their lego-crawlers or biomimetic final projects.

- [Paper recapping ME112 final projects](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-63537-8_47)
- [Video of typical mid-term crawler project (TA-built trial-run)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZCVXoLwg)
